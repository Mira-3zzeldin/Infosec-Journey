## Answer Key & Explanations

**1. C — 67.6mm.** A standard SODIMM measures `67.6mm (2.6")` long by `32mm (1.25")` tall, dramatically smaller than a full-size desktop DIMM to fit within a laptop's constrained case.

**2. C — 260.** DDR4 SODIMMs use a `260-pin` configuration, sitting between DDR3's `204-pin` and DDR5's `262-pin` designs in the memory generation progression covered in the Chart Room section.

**3. C — 262.** DDR5 SODIMMs use `262 pins`, the newest and highest pin count in the laptop memory lineup, supporting capacities up to `64 GB` per module as of this chapter's writing.

**4. C — 45.5mm.** A MicroDIMM measures roughly `45.5mm (1.75")` long by `30mm (1.2")` wide — over 50 percent smaller than a SODIMM, and noticeably more square in profile.

**5. B — Flip Chip Ball Grid Array.** Micro-FCBGA is a solder-ball-based chip mounting standard used to attach laptop processors directly to the motherboard, replacing the pin-and-socket approach common on desktop CPUs and generally eliminating any possibility of a processor upgrade.

**6. C — 52.** Mini PCIe cards connect via a `52-pin` card edge connector, distinguishing them from M.2's `66-pin` connector despite both technologies serving similar expansion roles.

**7. C — 66.** M.2 uses a `66-pin` connector — more pins than Mini PCIe's 52, despite M.2's physically narrower `22mm` width compared to Mini PCIe's `30mm`.

**8. C — 30mm × 51mm.** Full-sized Mini PCIe cards measure `30mm` wide by `51mm` long; half-sized cards measure `30mm × 27mm` instead.

**9. B — 10 centimeters.** NFC's effective range tops out around `10 centimeters (about 4")`, which is precisely why it's difficult (though not impossible) to intercept an NFC transaction from any meaningful distance.

**10. A — K-lock.** A cable lock is also widely known as a **Kensington lock** or **K-lock**, named after the company that popularized the anchor-cable laptop security design.

**11. D — 2022.** Apple began shifting its product line from the proprietary Lightning connector toward USB-C starting in 2022, though Lightning was expected to remain common on existing devices for several more years afterward.

**12. B — Acrylonitrile Butadiene Styrene.** ABS (or an ABS composite) is the most common plastic used for laptop cases, chosen over metals like aluminum or titanium primarily for cost, though some premium laptops still use those lightweight metals.

**13. D — Zinc-carbon (ZnC).** NiCd, Li-ion, and NiMH are cited as the three historically most popular laptop battery chemistries, with Li-poly gaining ground more recently; zinc-carbon is a disposable-battery chemistry not used in rechargeable laptop batteries.

**14. B — Around 2005.** Mini PCIe has served as the dominant laptop internal expansion slot standard since roughly 2005, eventually giving ground to the newer, higher-throughput M.2 standard.

**15. B — Li-ion.** Li-ion batteries are specifically associated with the "digital memory effect," where the battery's own charge-monitoring gauge becomes miscalibrated over time and reports an inaccurate remaining charge, rather than the battery's actual chemical capacity degrading.

**16. A — NiCd.** Nickel cadmium batteries are the classic example of true "chemical memory effect," where repeated partial discharge cycles measurably reduce the battery's usable capacity over time.

**17. C — USB-C.** The example docking station in this chapter connects to its host laptop via a single USB-C cable, reflecting the industry's broad shift away from proprietary docking connectors.

**18. A — Riser card / daughterboard.** A riser card (called an "I/O board" by Dell specifically) is a secondary circuit board that offloads certain circuitry from a laptop's cramped main motherboard, trading space savings for a more expensive whole-board replacement if that circuitry fails.

**19. B — Control Panel ➤ Tablet PC Settings ➤ Calibrate.** Windows touchscreen calibration is accessed through Control Panel's Tablet PC Settings, walking the user through tapping a series of onscreen crosshairs before saving the resulting calibration data.

**20. B — HP.** "Touchpad" is a trademarked term belonging to Hewlett-Packard specifically, even though the word is commonly used generically across the industry to describe any laptop trackpad.

**21. B — In the BIOS/UEFI.** Shared video memory allocation for an integrated laptop GPU is configured directly in the system's BIOS/UEFI, commonly within a configurable range such as 512 MB to 2 GB.

**22. B — Total RAM available to the OS decreases by the newly allocated amount.** Because shared video memory is carved directly out of total system RAM, increasing the video allocation directly reduces how much RAM remains available for the operating system and applications.

**23. B — M-keyed M.2 slots support PCIe x4, while Mini PCIe is limited to PCIe x1.** While the slowest M.2 slots only reach PCIe x2, M-keyed M.2 slots support PCIe x4 — well beyond Mini PCIe's PCIe x1 ceiling — making the fastest M.2 implementations substantially quicker.

**24. B — Laptop motherboards and cases are proprietary, custom-fit designs rather than standardized, interchangeable parts.** Unlike desktops, laptop motherboards, video circuitry, and cases are designed together as a single custom-fit unit, so there's no consumer market for mixing components from different manufacturers into a self-built laptop.

**25. B — Both the old and new drives must be accessible to the migration software simultaneously.** Migration software generally requires simultaneous access to both drives — achieved by mounting the old drive in a second computer, a spare expansion slot, or via a USB-to-SATA adapter — in order to move files, settings, and applications together.

**26. B — Manual copying does not transfer installed applications or system/app settings.** Manually copying files moves only the raw data itself; any installed applications must be reinstalled from scratch, and configuration settings (like desktop layout or printer setup) must be manually reconfigured on the new system.

**27. C — Revisiting the BIOS/UEFI to reconfigure the video memory allocation.** Since a RAM upgrade changes the total memory pool that shared video memory draws from, the BIOS/UEFI's video allocation setting should be revisited and reconfigured to reflect the new total.

**28. B — Lower voltage/clock speed manages heat output within a laptop's confined, poorly ventilated case.** Because laptop cases offer far less room for airflow than a desktop tower, engineers deliberately run laptop CPUs at lower voltages and clock speeds specifically to keep heat output manageable, accepting a performance tradeoff in exchange.

**29. B — The motherboard and OS working together to reduce CPU speed/power draw when full performance isn't needed, then restoring it on demand.** Processor throttling is a coordinated, dynamic behavior — not a permanent lock — that scales CPU performance up or down based on real-time demand and power source (battery vs. plugged in).

**30. B — To recalibrate the battery gauge, correcting the digital memory effect that causes inaccurate charge readings.** A monthly full discharge specifically targets the gauge-calibration problem behind the Li-ion digital memory effect, distinct from any actual chemical degradation of the battery itself.

**31. B — It originally referred specifically to a wired USB connection and has broadened to include wireless connections as well.** The term "tethering" historically meant connecting to a hotspot via a physical USB cable specifically, though common usage today includes wireless tethering connections as well.

**32. A — A hotspot shares a cellular connection wirelessly (potentially to multiple devices), while tethering historically referred to a single wired connection.** A hotspot broadcasts a device's cellular connection over Wi-Fi (often to multiple connected devices), while tethering specifically described a single device connecting to that shared connection, historically via USB cable.

**33. C — The specific connection method used to attach to the laptop (proprietary port vs. universal USB-C, for example).** With functionality now nearly identical between the two, the one remaining meaningful technical distinction is how each device physically connects to the laptop — a legacy proprietary docking port versus a modern universal USB-C connection.

**34. B — A lower-wattage adapter may be unable to reliably power and charge the laptop under load.** Choosing an adapter rated below the original's wattage risks insufficient power delivery under real-world load, which is why replacements should always match or exceed the original wattage rating.

**35. A — Because installed applications and personalized configuration settings won't transfer with a simple file copy, requiring reinstalls/reconfiguration or dedicated migration software.** Even a typical home user's setup usually includes installed apps and personalized settings beyond raw files, and those specifically require either manual reinstallation/reconfiguration or dedicated migration software to carry over.

**36. A — Processor throttling.** Processor throttling is the specific mechanism by which the motherboard and OS coordinate to lower CPU speed and power draw, directly extending battery runtime when full performance isn't required.

**37. B — The trackpad sits directly below the keyboard, exactly where a typist's resting palms make contact.** The trackpad's physical placement, directly beneath the keys where palms naturally rest while typing, is the direct cause of accidental cursor-jumping input during normal typing.

**38. A — SSDs are faster, produce less heat, and are significantly more resistant to the physical shock of everyday portable use.** These three advantages — speed, lower heat, and shock resistance — are the specific reasons SSDs dominate laptop storage today, despite carrying a higher cost per gigabyte than spinning hard drives.

**39. B — Internal space is prioritized for other components, with external USB optical drives available when needed instead.** Modern laptops simply don't have room to spare for an internal optical drive, so when optical media access is genuinely needed, an external USB optical drive fills that gap instead, at a modest speed tradeoff.

**40. B — The technician is attempting to install a SODIMM generation (e.g., DDR4) into a slot designed for a different, incompatible generation (e.g., DDR3).** Different SODIMM generations differ from one another by only a few millimeters, but those small dimensional differences are exactly what prevent an incompatible generation from physically seating in the wrong slot.

**41. B — Reseat the battery by disconnecting it, waiting a few seconds, and reconnecting it.** This exact symptom pattern (won't power on battery-only, works fine plugged in, battery shows a charge) is specifically called out as often resolvable with a simple reseat before escalating to a full battery replacement.

**42. B — The trackpad is picking up accidental palm contact; disable or reduce its sensitivity.** Random cursor jumps specifically while typing are the signature symptom of palm contact on a trackpad positioned directly below the keyboard, and adjusting sensitivity or disabling it is the appropriate first response.

**43. B — Whether the BIOS/UEFI's shared video memory allocation needs to be reconfigured for the new RAM total.** Because shared video memory draws from the total system RAM pool, a RAM upgrade without revisiting that BIOS/UEFI allocation can leave the system under-provisioned for either general use or graphics performance.

**44. B — Replace the entire keyboard assembly, since most laptop keyboards don't support single-key replacement.** Unlike some desktop mechanical keyboards, laptop keyboards generally require full-assembly replacement rather than individual key repair once liquid damage causes sticking or unresponsive keys.

**45. B — The technician purchased a SODIMM generation that differs from what the slot supports (e.g., DDR4 into a DDR3 slot).** Successive SODIMM generations differ from each other by only a few millimeters in physical dimension specifically to prevent an incompatible generation from being force-fit into the wrong slot.

**46. B — Recalibrate the touchscreen via Control Panel ➤ Tablet PC Settings.** Inconsistent touch registration, especially concentrated at the screen's edges, is the classic symptom that touchscreen calibration is meant to fix, and should be attempted before considering a hardware replacement.

**47.
